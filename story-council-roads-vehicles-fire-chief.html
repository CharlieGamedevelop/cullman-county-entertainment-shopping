Hanceville council adds $25,000 for road patching, backs mayor on fire chief

Published Saturday, October 17, 2026 by Cullman County Today

More gas tax money is going toward potholes, a take home vehicle policy is still up for debate, and the council upheld the mayor's removal of the fire chief. The next meeting is Thursday, Oct. 22, at 6 p.m.

Potholes were near the top of the list when the Hanceville City Council met on Oct. 8. Council members approved another $25,000 from gas tax money so Public Works can keep patching streets, according to The Cullman Tribune.

The job has turned out bigger than planned. Rusty Fields of Public Works said the damaged spots vary a lot in size and depth, which takes extra time and material. City and county crews are working together at a cost of about $5,000 to $5,500 a day. Fields told the council they are a little more than halfway through.

Take home trucks took up the most time. Councilman Robert Powell has proposed a policy, Resolution 2026-8, that would limit everyday take home vehicles to department heads and employees who are on call. He pointed to fuel, insurance and upkeep, and one estimate put six take home vehicles at roughly $17,000 a year.

Public Works employees pushed back. They said having a truck at home gets them to downed trees, flooding and storm damage faster, and some keep their own tools in those trucks. The council added emergency exceptions and a six month phase out, then held the final vote for its next meeting.

The council also talked about having a say in hiring the fire chief, public works director and park director. Mayor Nolan Bradford said he wants to keep that authority with the mayor. No action was taken.

After about 40 minutes in executive session, the council voted unanimously to uphold Bradford's removal of Fire Chief Chris Hardin, who started in March. The Tribune reported that no reason was given in public and no replacement was named. The Cullman Times reported the vote as well.

A few other notes from the meeting: firefighters answered 116 calls in September, the city gave $500 to the North Alabama Agriplex, Councilman Cameron Rowe joined the Planning Commission, and work is moving ahead on policies needed to bring back a city police department.

If you want to speak up, the next council meeting is Thursday, Oct. 22, at 6 p.m.
