Public Safety

Sheriff's environmental deputy clears up to 35 tons of roadside litter a year

Published Friday, October 23, 2026 by Cullman County Today

Deputy Ron Wood picks up an estimated 50,000 to 70,000 pounds of trash along about 100 miles of county roads each year, and recycling money goes to local nonprofits.

If a county road near you looks cleaner than it used to, Environmental Deputy Ron Wood may be the reason. The Cullman County Sheriff's Office says Wood collects an estimated 50,000 to 70,000 pounds of roadside litter every year, which works out to roughly 25 to 35 tons from about 100 miles of roadway.

Sheriff Matt Gentry and Wood talked about the program in a video the office shared this week. Recyclable items pulled from the roadsides are sold, and that money is donated to nonprofit groups around Cullman County.

The litter program has been running for years. Back in 2018, a crew Wood supervised gathered more than 51,000 pounds of trash from 185 miles of county roads, and the aluminum cans alone raised over $1,300 for Cullman Caring for Kids.

The sheriff's office says the work has earned it yearly recognition from Alabama People Against a Littered State, the statewide group known as Alabama PALS, whose Governor's Awards include a category for law enforcement.

Gentry praised Wood's dedication and said the program keeps roads clean, protects the environment, and helps local nonprofits. You can do your part by securing loads in truck beds and keeping trash inside the vehicle until you reach a can.
